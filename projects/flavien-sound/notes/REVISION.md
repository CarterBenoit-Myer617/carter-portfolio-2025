## Abstract
A pair of animated concert posters for Flavien Berger that capture his dreamy, confident sound through bold, childlike illustration and vibrant color — designed to stand out against Montreal's industrial spring landscape. The series pairs simple, playful gestures with motion graphics to reflect the personality of an artist whose music carries a quiet cult following.

## System Logic
The two-poster system maps directly to the experience of the trip. The first poster features a scuba diver animation — referencing the waterfront and the island venue where the concert took place. The second is a bright yellow animated car that spins and eventually breaks down in a frame-by-frame, stop-motion glitch — echoing the urban road-trip energy of getting there. Together, the pair uses the real setting as visual source material without turning the project into a travelogue. Supplementary stickers and small-format pieces extend the series without overloading it.

## Cultural Context
Montreal in the spring is a city waking up — more community engagement, more event promotion, more street-level energy after a long winter. Flavien Berger's music already carries a dreamy, almost surreal confidence, and the posters lean into that by using bold, saturated color and simple strokes that feel out of place against the city's concrete and steel. That contrast is the point: vibrant, childlike graphics in an industrial setting create a juxtaposition that draws attention and mirrors the feeling of arriving somewhere new with fresh eyes. The journalistic approach — using real footage and imagery from the trip as a backdrop — gives viewers context for where the event took place without making the project about the vacation itself.

## Outcome / Proof
The project reinforced a key lesson in confidence and simplicity: a poster doesn't need to be overthought. A single bold stroke and a clear vision can speak more truthfully to the artist and their music than a complex composition. By trusting the gesture and letting the work stay simple, the posters communicate Flavien Berger's sound more directly than a more polished approach would.

## Presentation Philosophy
Keep it small and simple. Show just enough that the story is told effectively and people want to see more. Don't overdo mockups for something this subtle — the posters and animations speak for themselves. The goal is authenticity: people should see this as a real event that actually happened, not a polished case study exercise.

## Key Design Decisions
- Two-poster pair: scuba diver (waterfront/island) + yellow car with stop-motion breakdown (urban/road trip)
- Journalistic aesthetic using trip footage as backdrop, NOT a vacation vlog
- Bold, vibrant color against Montreal's industrial concrete setting
- Childlike, playful strokes that reflect the dreamy quality of the music
- Confidence in simplicity — trust the stroke, let the work speak for the artist
- Less is more — don't oversaturate with mockups, let the work breathe
