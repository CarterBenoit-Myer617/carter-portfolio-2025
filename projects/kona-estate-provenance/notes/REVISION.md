## Abstract
KONA is a luxury Hawaiian coffee brand built around cultural preservation, sustainability, and the ritual of pour-over coffee. The project spans package design, brand identity, and digital experience — revitalizing a market historically seen as cheap commodity coffee by grounding the brand in the authentic heritage of Hawaiian coffee farming without culturally appropriating or expropriating that heritage.

## System Logic
The packaging system is built around a single folded sheet of paper — origami-inspired — that forms the entire coffee bag structure. This serves as both a sustainable design solution and a reference to traditional Hawaiian weaving patterns. The exterior is strong and durable (as luxury packaging should be), while everything inside — bags, filter packs — is fully biodegradable. The package is designed to be reused, making it gift-worthy and shelf-worthy beyond the coffee itself. Colors and pattern styles are pulled directly from the Hawaiian landscape — raw, natural materials translated into a controlled, sophisticated visual language through the brand's marketing system.

## Cultural Context
Hawaiian Kona coffee has been undervalued in the broader market — often seen as just another commodity rather than the craft product it is. This project works to increase its cultural capital in the beverage space by telling the true story: the heritage of the farming, the land it comes from, the care in the process. The design avoids exoticizing or appropriating Hawaiian culture — instead, it references the real landscape and traditional craft (weaving patterns, natural colors) as source material, letting the coffee's own character do the heavy lifting. The brand positions pour-over coffee as a morning ritual, not a convenience — the opposite of an espresso pod. You take the time. You appreciate the pure ingredients. That slowness and intentionality is the luxury.

## Outcome / Proof
The project demonstrates how graphic design and visual communication can revitalize a market perception — taking something seen as cheap and repositioning it as premium through authentic storytelling, sustainable innovation, and cultural respect. The origami packaging structure proves that sustainability and luxury aren't in conflict. The brand system shows restraint: letting the natural notes of the coffee and the real landscape of Hawaii speak, rather than over-designing the message.

## Key Design Decisions
- Single folded paper (origami) bag structure — sustainable + references Hawaiian weaving
- Exterior: durable, luxurious, reusable / Interior: fully biodegradable
- Color and pattern palette sourced from Hawaiian landscape and raw natural materials
- No cultural appropriation — design references heritage through material and craft, not stereotypes
- Pour-over ritual positioning — luxury is in the slowness and intentionality, not just price
- Gift-worthy packaging that lives beyond the product
- Digital expansion (web/app) to show range and support a subscription model
